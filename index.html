The coaching approach

Technique matters. Timing makes it usable.

I build the skill, then connect it to the read that tells you when and why to use it.

01

READ

See the play before the ball arrives.

Track the server, setter, hitter, block, and available space so movement starts from information instead of panic.

02

MOVE

Arrive balanced enough to make choices.

Footwork is not just speed. It is getting your body behind the ball while preserving control and multiple options.

03

CONTROL

Turn contact into a repeatable outcome.

Own the platform, angle, tempo, and decision so the next touch becomes easier for your team.

What I bring to the table

A lifetime of playing, developing players, and learning how different athletes improve.

Volleyball has been part of my life since middle school. I have experienced the game as a setter, libero, collegiate player, player-development leader, teammate, tournament competitor, and coach. That range helps me understand both what a player needs to do and how to explain it in a way that actually clicks.

01

Experience across positions and levels

I started playing in middle school, set for my high school team, played libero in club, and continued playing for five years at UWM. That gives me a setter's view of court organization and a libero's understanding of first contact, defense, and reading the game.

02

Player development leadership

I served for two and a half years on the Milwaukee Gay Volleyball Association board as Player Development Director, helping create opportunities for players to learn, improve, and become more confident competitors.

03

Proven beginner development

I have successfully coached players and teams from zero experience to competent, confident volleyball. I meet athletes where they are, identify the next skill that matters, and build progress without overwhelming them.

04

Competitive results

After countless matches and tournaments, I understand what holds up under pressure. I have coached adult-league athletes and teams who went on to win medals, not just look better during drills.

05

Coaching that adapts to the athlete

Different players need different cues, progressions, and levels of challenge. My goal is not to force everyone into one template. It is to understand where you are, where you want to go, and what will move you forward fastest.

06

An honest network for specialized goals

Good coaching includes knowing when an athlete needs a different specialty. When a goal falls outside what I can deliver at the right level, I can connect or collaborate with experienced and professional volleyball players in my network.
